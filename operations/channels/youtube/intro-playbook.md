# Intro Playbook

The intro is not a per-video creative decision. It is a **grammar**: a fixed 30-second beat grid, a fill-in writing template, two ritual closers, and one reusable title card. Every video fills in the blanks; nothing else gets invented.

> Why: scripting stalls at the intro because each one is being designed from scratch. A grammar turns "write an intro" into a 30-minute fill-in task — and guarantees the footage exists when the edit needs it.

---

## The core insight (zero-subscriber math)

At zero subscribers the finished object is the channel's **only credential** — no face equity, no catchphrase equity, no mission equity. Every second before the viewer sees proof of the payoff is spent on credit the channel doesn't have.

So invert the 006 note: not *"chaos, then result"* but **result, then chaos**. Cold traffic gives an unknown channel ~5–10 seconds before the first exit wave; the fastest way to survive it is visual proof that the promised thing exists and works.

**Rule: shot one is the thumbnail, in motion.** Design thumbnail and first shot as a single unit.

---

## Default grid — build videos (006, 002, 003, 007, 008)

Total: **30 seconds.** One tone, no music until the card.

| Time | Beat | Rules |
|------|------|-------|
| 0:00–0:04 | **The proof.** Finished thing mid-function, macro/top-down, real sound only. One dry VO line — flat, factual, no adjectives. | Pays off the thumbnail inside 4 seconds. No logo, no greeting, no music. |
| 0:04–0:12 | **The rewind.** Hard cut to the before-state. Exactly 3 shots, 2–3s each, handheld, uglier light. | The contrast IS the hook: "how did THAT become THIS?" |
| 0:12–0:22 | **The stakes number.** One honest, *actually measured* number that quantifies the problem (stopwatch, lux meter, price), plus one sentence of why it matters. | Face-on allowed here, max 5 seconds static. |
| 0:22–0:28 | **The vow + promise.** "So — let's build our own." Then a 3-shot flash-forward (1.5–2s each) of the process — **one of the three must be a failure frame**, with an honest-limits line. | Showing the plan, never announcing it. The failure frame plants a minute-5 retention hook. |
| 0:28–0:30 | **The card.** 2 seconds max. Navy `#06224c`, Space Grotesk semibold title, amber `#ffab40` ping, sonic logo (click → ping). Music enters here, not one frame earlier. | Below the title, permanently, in small type: *eurísko (εὑρίσκω) — Greek: "I find out."* |

## Variant grid — concept / test / failure videos (001, 004, 005, software)

Same 30-second envelope, same card. Use when there's no beautiful finished object to lead with.

| Time | Beat | Rules |
|------|------|-------|
| 0:00–0:03 | **The claim.** One falsifiable, slightly absurd-but-true sentence, delivered completely flat over a single mid-action shot. Ambient sound only. | 005 already has it: fan screaming, deadpan — "This thing could heat a house." |
| 0:03–0:15 | **The evidence burst.** 3–4 shots, 2–4s each; **every shot contains a readable number or label on screen**. | Must survive muted autoplay — a big share of suggested-feed plays are silent. |
| 0:15–0:25 | **The question.** Convert the claim into the driving question + method in one breath. First face-on beat here (max 5s). Must echo the title/thumbnail wording by 0:25. | |
| 0:25–0:30 | **The closer + card.** "One way to find out." — amber ping fires on the words *find out*. Card as above. | The phrase promises investigation, not success — the no-overclaiming rule as a catchphrase. |

## The two ritual closers

Same card, same sonic logo, two spoken lines keyed to video type:

- **Build videos:** *"So — let's build our own."* (already a brand keyword)
- **Tests / concepts / deliberate failures:** *"One way to find out."* (the channel name is the catchphrase — eurísko, root of *eureka*; nobody can copy it without copying the name)

Both delivered dry, followed by a **hard cut** — never by an explanation of the plan.

---

## Script template (fill this in on day 1, before any CAD)

Put this at the top of every video's storyboard and fill it at project kickoff — it's the actual cure for the blank page, because all five slots can be written before the build exists:

```
MISSION:   I'm trying to ______. I can't yet, because ______. So today: ______.
CLAIM:     (one flat, falsifiable sentence a stranger would want disproven)
NUMBER:    (the measured value that quantifies the problem — measure it FOR REAL, on camera)
QUESTION:  (the engineering question the video answers, echoing the title wording)
DONE WHEN: (falsifiable success criterion, e.g. "all six drawers close" — this is also the video's ending)
CLOSER:    let's build our own / one way to find out
```

## Day-1 capture checklist (footage debt prevention)

Half the grid depends on footage that only exists if capture starts on day 1. Standing checklist per project:

- [ ] Locked wide of the before-state from a marked tripod position (tape the floor — enables the *Same Frame, Weeks Apart* match cut later as a mid-video act break or end-screen shot)
- [ ] The problem happening for real, sync sound (the failed search, the screaming fan, the murky water)
- [ ] The number being measured, on camera (stopwatch, lux meter, thermal cam)
- [ ] Failures as they happen — never delete a failed print/test clip; the promise beat needs one
- [ ] The money shot when finished: the single most satisfying 8 seconds the built thing performs, macro, real sound — this is shot one AND the thumbnail

## Hard rules

1. **Numbers are data, not writing devices.** The whole brand is honesty — the moment a viewer suspects the stopwatch is a prop, it collapses. Measure once, for real, on camera, and use that value everywhere.
2. **No adjectives in the hook.** The shot sells; the line labels. Deadpan is the structural opposite of hype.
3. **Show, never announce.** (Your own 006 note.) The vow is a decision, not a roadmap.
4. **Amber appears in the intro only on the card / ritual ping** — "amber = the eureka moment" becomes enforceable grammar.
5. **Music enters at the title card, never before.** The silence-then-music flip is the re-hook.
6. **Re-hook after the card.** The second-biggest exit spike is right after the title card — the first post-card line must open a new question, not settle in. Any personal/context material runs over b-roll, static talking head capped at 5 seconds.
7. **Title/thumbnail echo by 0:25** at the latest.

## Worked example — 006 Tool Organization (channel opener)

```
0:00–0:04
V: Macro, top-down. A drill drops into a matte 3D-printed inlay pocket — perfect fit,
   soft thunk. Cut: the drawer glides shut, every tool seated in its own pocket.
A: (dry, workshop ambience only) "Every tool in this drawer has exactly one place
   it can physically be."

0:04–0:12
V: Hard cut, handheld, harsher light. Shot 1: a drawer jammed open by a clamp.
   Shot 2: digging through a bin of tangled cables for a hex key. Shot 3: the bench
   buried under tools, a mug, a filament spool.
A: "Three weeks ago, nothing did. Same workshop. Same drawers."

0:12–0:22
V: Locked-off shot: searching drawer after drawer for a caliper. On-screen stopwatch,
   footage sped up, freeze on the REAL measured time.
A: "I timed it — [X] minutes to find a caliper I'd used the day before. I'm trying to
   build products I can actually sell. A shop where the tools hide from you doesn't
   produce anything."

0:22–0:28
V: (to camera, one beat) — then flash-forward, 3 cuts: parametric CAD reshaping around
   a wrench outline / printer time-lapse / a too-tight test inlay CRACKING as the
   wrench is forced in.
A: "So — let's build our own. One parametric file, any tool, any drawer. The first
   version broke. Here's the whole process."

0:28–0:30
V: Card: TOOL ORGANIZATION — navy, Space Grotesk, amber ping.
   Small: eurísko (εὑρίσκω) — "I find out."
A: Click → ping. Music starts here.

0:30–1:30 — personal context beat, ALWAYS over b-roll (carrying tools, whiteboard
sketch, pan across printers):
A: "Quick context, since we've never met. I'm Gabriel — engineer. This room is
   supposed to become a one-person product company: software, hardware, the printers
   behind me. The first sellable product is already on the drawing board. But you
   just watched me lose a caliper for [X] minutes — nothing ships out of this room
   until the room itself works. So that's the actual first project: the workshop."
→ then into research ("what inlay systems already exist?").
   This footage also cuts into the 60s channel trailer.
```

## Worked example — 002 Therapy Lamp (fixing the drafted intro)

```
0:00–0:05
V: Night. Pitch-dark desk, only monitor glow. A hand turns the dial on the finished
   lamp — the desk floods with daylight-white light, exposure blooms and settles.
   One unbroken shot. (The "click, then quiet" beat from the old draft, promoted
   to shot one.)
A: (quiet, dry) "This is the closest thing to sunlight I could legally build."

0:05–0:12
V: Hard cut — grey reality. Phone alarm 6:58 in a dark bedroom / sleet on a train
   window / flickering office fluorescents. (The best 3 shots of the old 9-beat
   stress montage; the rest is cut.)
A: "In a Swiss December you get about eight hours of daylight. I spend almost all
   of them indoors."

0:12–0:22
V: Fast graphic: sun's spectrum vs. tanning-bed spectrum — the UVB sliver
   highlighted amber on the sun side, collapsing to nothing on the tanning-bed side.
A: "The usual fixes are worse than they look. Supplements have quality-control
   problems. And tanning beds are the strange one — you can get the cancer risk
   without the vitamin D. That's real, and the chart that proves it is coming."

0:22–0:28
V: Flash-forward: soldering the full-spectrum strip / lux meter climbing past
   10,000 / the hinge failing mid-test, the head drooping onto the desk.
A: "So — let's build our own. The science of why bright light works on your brain,
   the build — and the one thing this lamp can't do."

0:28–0:30
V: Card. Click → ping, music in.
→ Straight into science Part 1 ("why do we get sick in winter?").
```

**Disposition of the drafted 002 opening:**
- **Keep:** the lamp click-on / grey-turns-to-color moment (promoted to 0:00 — the single best retention shot in the plan) and the honest question *"how can I get the sunlight I need without getting cancer or breaking the bank?"* (must land by ~0:25, not minute 3).
- **Cut entirely:** the snowy-ridge / cabin / green-screen-fireplace ASMR sequence. It's a skit in a third tone, days of solo production, and 40+ seconds of no-narration atmosphere is a mass-exit event at zero subscribers. Fake-cozy green-screen footage also undercuts a brand whose currency is realness. (Optional: bring the fireplace back as a 5-second payoff at the very end, when the finished lamp makes the room feel warm for real.)
- **Relocate:** the fireplace monologue's vitamin D / tanning-bed content is genuinely good — it IS the science segment (Parts 1–7 already in the storyboard), delivered over graphics at minute 1+, where curiosity is already banked.
- **Compress:** stress montage 9 beats → best 3 shots, 6–8 seconds, as the rewind beat.

## Worked example — 005 PU Heating (concept variant)

```
0:00–0:03
V: Couch. Laptop on lap, fan at full scream. Look at camera, completely flat.
A: (over the roaring fan, no music) "This thing could heat a house."

0:03–0:15
V: Evidence burst. Thermal-cam view of the laptop, vents blooming white-orange —
   on-screen: "94 °C". Space heater spec label — "2000 W". GPU on the bench —
   "450 W each". Hand flat on the living-room radiator.
A: "Not a joke. Every watt a computer draws leaves it as heat — physics doesn't
   allow anything else. A computer is a space heater that does math on the way.
   Your radiator does the same job, minus the math."

0:15–0:25
V: Whiteboard: house cross-section, radiators erased, little server racks drawn in.
   Cut: data-center exhaust vents steaming.
A: "Data centers pay millions to throw this heat away, while we pay to make identical
   heat with dumb resistors. So: could a rack of processors actually heat a home —
   and would anyone pay you for the math it does on the way?"

0:25–0:30
V: (to thermal camera, own face glowing in thermal palette, one beat)
A: "One way to find out."
V: Card: PROCESSING UNITS AS HEATING. Amber ping on "find out". Click → ping.
A: "Let's run the numbers."
```

## Where the discarded ideas went

- *Same Frame, Weeks Apart* (locked-tripod match cut, DAY 1 → DAY 21) — demoted from intro to **filming discipline + mid-video act break / end-screen shot**. Tape the tripod position on day 1 of every project regardless.
- *The Wrong Answer First* (store-bought thing failing on camera) — superb, but it's a **minute-2 body segment**, not a cold open: it retains viewers who are already in. Use it right after the context beat (006's "why not foam inserts?", 002's lux-meter test of a commercial lamp).
- *The Blocker*'s mission skeleton ("I'm trying to ___...") — lives in the script template and the personal context beat, not the hook slot.
