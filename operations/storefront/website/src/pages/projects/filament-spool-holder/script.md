one common issue if you print with materials like petg, tpu, nylon etc is moisture, this materials are hygroscopic meaning that they absorb moisture from surrounding air, this causes the  moisture to evaporate when the filament is extruded which can lead to crackling and bubbles in milder cases and full clogs or failed prints when the moisture is high. thus come the filament dryers, wether you bake your filament in the oven, use a dehydrator, put your filament in a storage box with silica packs or buy a commercial filament dryer, they all do the same thing.

now you might be asking gabriel i am only printing with pla why do i care about drying and you ve got point materials like pla, abs, asa are less pron to absorbing a lot of moisture and can usually be printed without drying before even if they are siting out for a while. so in order to earn its keep we need to think further. on obvious benefit of keeping your filament in a box is that it stays clean and dust free but thats not enough, thats when i had the thought what if i put in some sensors that transmit their data to the 3d printer thus allowing the printer to tune and adapt to the properties of each individual roll of filament.
first the obvious part a hydrometer and a thermometer, it can be used to warn you if the filament is to wet when you want to print something.
